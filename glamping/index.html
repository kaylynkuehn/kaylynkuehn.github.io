      How lots are graded

      Same rules for past sales and for current candidates. Budget logic: $50K all-in with about $25K to build.

      A True gem3+ acres for $30K or less, no warning notes, in the Catskills or Adirondacks, and it has a draw: water named in the listing, or it sits in a destination town (list below). Any property with an existing structure (camp, cottage, cabin, even a teardown) on 1+ acre for $50K or less also counts: the spot is already cleared and usually has a driveway, and often power, a well or septic. B Strong buyHas a draw but misses on size or price: water at any price up to $50K, or a destination town at $30K or less or with 3+ acres. Or pure value: dry land of 5+ acres for $25K or less and no more than 60% of FMV. C OrdinarySold for $50K or less with no warning notes, but nothing special. The baseline to compare against. FlaggedThe listing warns it may lack road access or frontage, or is wetland or underwater. Usually cheap for a reason. Over budgetSold for more than $50K.

      The Why column says what earned an A or B: Water, Location (destination town), Structure (an existing building) or Value. Water is not required, but a view is not in the listings, so a great-view lot outside a destination town can still land at C. Terms: FMV is the county's full market value estimate. High bid is the winning bid when bidding closed. % of FMV is high bid divided by FMV.

      Limits: price is the high bid when bidding closed, not a confirmed closing. Water only counts when the listing names it; 2023 and 2024 Sullivan catalogs did not label water, so gems there may be under-counted. A view is not in the listings. Camp condition is unknown.
